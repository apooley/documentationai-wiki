## Monitoring your carbonation

A carbonation run takes hours to days, and most of it happens unattended. The FizzWizz gives you three ways to keep an eye on it: the front panel, the web dashboard, and the mobile app.

## On the front panel

The display shows the three values that drive the carbonation calculation:

| Field | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **CO₂ reg psi** | Pressure at the CO₂ inlet. If this drops, your CO₂ supply is running low |
| **Brite psi** | Pressure in the arm/head transfer circuit — the tank head space pressure |
| **Temperature** | Product temperature from the WizzTemp probe |

During a normal carbonation run, expect **Brite psi to climb steadily** toward the finish pressure while **temperature stays stable**. A temperature that drifts shifts the target pressure with it; the FizzWizz recalculates automatically, but a large drift is worth investigating.

For a live, updating view of all three values plus manual solenoid control, use [Test Mode](/troubleshooting/health-check/test-mode).

## On the dashboard

The [BrewWizz dashboard](/dashboard/overview) is the main monitoring tool for day-to-day use. It runs at [brewwizzdata.onesmartview.com/auth/login](https://brewwizzdata.onesmartview.com/auth/login) and shows:

- **Device health** — whether each FizzWizz is reporting, and how recently
- **Telemetry charts** — pressure and temperature over time
- **Alert rules and alert history** — what has fired and when
- **Recent readings** — the latest values from each device

Because the dashboard keeps historical data, it is the best place to answer questions like *"when did the pressure stop climbing?"* or *"has the temperature been drifting all week?"*. See [Device Detail & Charts](/dashboard/device-detail) for how to read the charts.

## On the mobile app

The [BrewWizz mobile app](/mobile-app/overview) brings device health, alerts, and account settings to your phone, with the same health states and alert history as the dashboard. It is currently in development — the Mobile App section describes planned features.

## What to watch for

<Callout kind="tip">

These are the signals that most often precede a problem. Each one links to the relevant diagnosis.

</Callout>

| Signal | Likely meaning | Where to go |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CO₂ reg psi falling** | CO₂ supply running low, or a regulator problem | [Responding to Alerts](/troubleshooting/responding-to-alerts) |
| **Brite psi not climbing** | A leak in the arm or stone circuit | [Carbonation & Brite Pressure](/troubleshooting/carbonation-and-brite-pressure) |
| **Brite psi climbing unusually fast** | Leaking Head_Transfer solenoid, or a broken stone | [Check 6 — Leak Testing](/troubleshooting/health-check/check-6-leak-testing) |
| **Temperature reading implausible or drifting** | WizzTemp probe or internal loom | [Slushy WizzTemp Test](/troubleshooting/temperature-probe) |
| **Device showing Stale or Offline** | Power, network, or a firmware update | [Device Offline or Not Reporting](/troubleshooting/device-offline) |
| **Carbonation ends up below target** | Stone fouling, temperature error, or brite starting pressure | [Carbonation & Brite Pressure](/troubleshooting/carbonation-and-brite-pressure) |

## Using the data for quality control

Recorded telemetry can be kept as a quality record for each batch. Because the dashboard stores the full run, you can correlate a finished batch's carbonation result against the pressure and temperature trace it was produced under — useful for repeatability and for investigating a batch that came out off-target.
