## Slushy WizzTemp test

The Health Check (Ident C) confirms the temperature reading is plausible. This test confirms it is **accurate** by comparing against a `0°C / 32°F` ice-water reference — the only way to prove the probe is genuinely calibrated.

<Callout kind="info">

The FizzWizz must be in [Test Mode](/health-check/test-mode) before starting this test.

</Callout>

## Step 1: Make a slushy

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fill container with crushed ice">

  Use ice that is as finely crushed as possible. The smaller the pieces, the more complete the contact between ice and water.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Add water">

  Pour in approximately 20% additional tap water. Do not worry if it overflows — the important thing is to keep the ice.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill all gaps">

  Ensure all gaps between ice pieces are filled with water. The ice-water interface is exactly `0°C / 32°F` — this is the reference point you are testing against.

  </Step>
</Steps>

![Slushy preparation](/images/slushy-preparation.png)

*Target consistency: crushed ice with gaps filled by water.*

## Step 2: Agitate the sensor continuously

<Steps>
  <Step title="Insert the sensor">

  Place the WizzTemp sensor into the slushy.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Agitate vigorously">

  Continually and vigorously move the sensor — like mixing a cup of tea. Move it up and down and around.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep moving for 2–3 minutes">

  After `2–3` minutes, check the reading on the display, but do not stop moving the sensor.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Never stop during the test">

  Continuous agitation is required for the entire duration of the test. If you stop, the test becomes invalid.

  </Step>
</Steps>

![Sensor agitation](/images/slushy-sensor-agitation.png)

*Move the sensor continuously, up and down and around.*

## Why agitation matters

<Callout kind="alert">

A stationary probe forms a warmer meltwater layer around itself and reads high. Insufficient agitation is the most common reason the test appears to fail on a healthy probe. If your reading is above the pass threshold, agitate more vigorously and try again before suspecting the sensor.

</Callout>

## Expected results

| Reading | Result | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0.25°C / 32.45°F` or lower | Pass | Sensor is accurate. Exact threshold depends on water impurities. |
| Above `0.25°C / 32.45°F` | Fail — reading high | 1. Confirm vigorous agitation and fresh ice. 2. Then suspect the probe. |
| Below `−0.16°C / 31.71°F` | Fail — impossibly low | Faulty probe or internal loom. Replace WizzTemp sensor. |

![Display reading](/images/slushy-display-reading.png)

*A passing reading during the slushy test.*

## For more accurate testing

<Callout kind="tip">

Use deionized water instead of tap water. Without dissolved impurities, the freezing point is closer to exactly `0°C`, giving a tighter pass/fail band.

</Callout>

## If the test fails

1. Repeat the test properly — fresh slushy, fresh ice, vigorous agitation. Most "failures" are actually insufficient agitation.
2. Inspect the internal loom for damage or corrosion.
3. Change the WizzTemp sensor.

See Ident C in [Troubleshooting by Symptom](/troubleshooting-guide) for full diagnostic steps.

## Why this matters for carbonation

<Callout kind="info">

Carbonation volumes are calculated from temperature. A probe that reads warm causes systematic under-carbonation — the FizzWizz thinks the beer is warmer than it is and calculates less CO₂ than needed. See [Carbonation & Brite Pressure](/troubleshooting-carbonation) for the full chain of cause and effect.

</Callout>
