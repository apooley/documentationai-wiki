## What happens after purging?

Once the purge is complete, PacWizz records the pressure at completion and the time the purge took. It continues showing **current tank pressure** and **total elapsed time**.

These readings help you judge whether the assembled tank is holding pressure before transferring beverage into it. This is a **pressure-hold check**, not a certified tank pressure test.

## Read the four values

| Display value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Finish pressure** | Actual tank pressure recorded immediately after the purge finished |
| **Purge time** | How long the purge took |
| **Current brite pressure** | Tank pressure now |
| **Total elapsed time** | Time since the purge began, including the time after completion |

The **actual recorded finish pressure** may differ slightly from the value you selected in Purge SetUp. Use the recorded value when comparing readings.

## Estimate pressure loss per hour

First calculate the time **since completion**, not the entire time since the run began:

```text
Hold time = Total elapsed time − Purge time
Pressure loss = Recorded finish pressure − Current pressure
Pressure loss per hour = Pressure loss / Hold time in hours
```

If you work in minutes, multiply the loss per minute by 60 to get psi per hour.

### Worked example from the manual

- Pressure drop: **0.57 psi**.
- Time since the purge finished: **13 hours 42 minutes**.
- Hold time in minutes: `(13 × 60) + 42 = 822`.
- Pressure loss per hour: `(0.57 / 822) × 60 ≈ 0.0416 psi/hour`.

The manual rounds this to about **0.041 psi/hour**. These values illustrate the calculation only; they are **not** an acceptance limit or a recommended hold duration.

<Callout kind="alert">

Do not leave a tank **actively purging overnight**. The long elapsed-time example in the manual is not an instruction to run an unattended overnight purge. Follow your facility's supervision and pressure-hold procedures.

</Callout>

## Interpret the result carefully

- A falling pressure may indicate a leak, but **temperature changes also affect pressure**.
- A very short observation interval or small pressure change may be difficult to distinguish from normal measurement variation.
- There is no universal pass/fail pressure-loss threshold in the supplied manual. Use your facility's requirements and ask BrewWizz for advice if the loss is unexpected.
- The oxygen estimate does not establish that the tank is leak-free.

If the tank loses pressure unexpectedly, check the assembly and connections under your facility's safe isolation/depressurization procedure before transfer. Record the pressures, timers, settings, and any temperature change when [contacting support](/pacwizz/support-and-warranty).
