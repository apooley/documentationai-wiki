The BrewWizz Foot Pressure Sensor holds head space pressure more accurately during packaging and speeds up gas replacement, so packaging runs faster.

It can be installed in one of two locations:

- **At the bottom (foot) of the brite tank** (preferred). In this position the sensor reads the pressure the packaging line actually sees, so the pressure drop caused by hydrostatic pressure is eliminated and the packaging line gets a constant supply pressure.
- **On the CIP arm.**

<Callout kind="danger">Only use BrewWizz products with tanks that have a pressure relief valve (PRV) fitted. The PRV should be set to 15 psi.</Callout>

For using the FizzWizz or PacWizz in packaging mode, see the FizzWizz or PacWizz user manual.

## Compatibility

The Foot Pressure Sensor works with:

- PacWizz
- FizzWizz-BAE running **V2 code or newer**

If your FizzWizz-BAE is running earlier code, contact [info@brewwizz.com](mailto:info@brewwizz.com) about updating it.

## Connecting the sensor

- **FizzWizz:** plug the sensor into the **temperature sensor connector**. The FizzWizz detects the connected sensor automatically.
- **PacWizz:** plug the sensor into the dedicated **pressure sensor connector**.

### Switch box option (FizzWizz)

A FizzWizz uses the same connector for the temperature sensor and the Foot Pressure Sensor. The optional BrewWizz switch box sits between the FizzWizz and both sensors, so they can stay permanently connected and you select the active sensor with a toggle switch.

## Installation

Both mounting locations need a tri-clamp tee fitting, with the sensor clamped to the branch of the tee.

### At the foot of the brite tank

Install the tee between **two isolation valves**, one on each side of the sensor:

- **Tank-side isolation valve**, between the tank outlet and the sensor
- **Packaging-side isolation valve**, between the sensor and the packaging line

<Callout kind="danger">The tank-side isolation valve **must** be open while pressurising the tank. If it is closed, the PRV will trigger.</Callout>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the tank-side valve">
    With the packaging-side valve closed, open the tank-side isolation valve so the sensor reads tank pressure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pressurise the tank">
    Bring the brite tank up to the required foot pressure. See [Choosing a foot pressure](#choosing-a-foot-pressure).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start packaging">
    Once the tank has reached the required pressure, start the packaging line and open the packaging-side isolation valve.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Choosing a foot pressure

The sensor at the foot of the tank reads head space pressure **plus** the hydrostatic pressure of the product above it. Hydrostatic pressure is approximately **0.43 psi per foot** of liquid for water. It varies slightly with the specific gravity of your product.

To keep head space pressure at or above the carbonation finish pressure, set the foot pressure to at least:

**Minimum foot pressure = (product depth in ft × 0.43 psi) + finish pressure**

### Example

A product has a finish pressure of 10.5 psi once carbonation is complete, with 9 ft of product in the tank.

| Component | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Hydrostatic pressure (9 ft × 0.43 psi) | 3.87 psi |
| Finish pressure | 10.5 psi |
| **Minimum foot pressure** | **14.37 psi** |

### Why this matters

If head space pressure falls below the finish pressure during packaging, CO₂ starts to come out of solution. Once breakout starts, it tends to run away and is very difficult to stop.

If this happens, rest the brite tank for several hours with the FizzWizz in carbonation mode, so the CO₂ can re-dissolve at an appropriate equilibrium pressure. See [Carbonation & Brite Pressure](/troubleshooting-carbonation) for related troubleshooting.

### When the minimum is higher than the packaging line allows

Product temperature has a large effect on the pressure required.

In this example the packaging system needs 15 psi at the foot of the tank, but the finish pressure is 13 psi, with 9 ft of product:

| Component | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Hydrostatic pressure (9 ft × 0.43 psi) | 3.87 psi |
| Finish pressure | 13 psi |
| **Minimum foot pressure** | **16.87 psi** |

The 16.87 psi minimum is about 2 psi above what the packaging line needs. To bring it down, cool the product by approximately **1 °C (1.8 °F) for every psi of excess pressure**. Here that means cooling the product by about 2 °C (3.6 °F).

<Callout kind="alert">Most beers and wines freeze below −2 °C (28.4 °F). Do not cool the product so far that it freezes.</Callout>

## Cleaning

<Callout kind="danger">Do **not** submerge the sensor assembly. The sensor body has a small breathing hole under breathable fabric, and it is splash proof only. Submerging it can permanently damage the sensor.</Callout>

Clean **only** the tri-clamp face and the wetted sensing area at the process end. All metal parts are 316 stainless steel and can be cleaned with standard beverage cleaning products.
