---
title: Carbonation Stone Check Valve
description: Construction, installation, servicing, and chemical compatibility of the BrewWizz check valve for carbonation stones.
---

The BrewWizz check valve sits in the CO₂ line to the carbonation stone and prevents product from flowing back up the line. It is fully serviceable: the inner plug and duck bill valve can be replaced if worn or damaged.

## Construction

| Component | Material | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outer body | 304 stainless steel | Machined and polished |
| Inner plug | White Delrin (acetal) | Approved for direct food contact, replaceable |
| Duck bill valve | Silicone | Approved for direct food contact, replaceable |

**Connections:** both ends have standard ¼ in FNPT threads. Seal these connections with plumber's tape or a food-grade epoxy weld.

**Thread lock:** the inner plug is secured in the outer body with T42 SAF-T-LOK Blue (24202). Any medium-strength thread lock can be used instead, because the flange of the duck bill valve seals the inner plug threads from the beverage.

**Size:** approximately 2 3/8 × 1 7/16 in (6.0 × 3.7 cm).

**Cleaning:** the check valve can be cleaned using the [torpedo method](/hardware-setup/wizzstone-combo#torpedo-method). See [Chemical compatibility](#chemical-compatibility) before choosing a cleaner.

## Installation

<Callout kind="alert">The check valve must be installed **vertically**, as shown in the assembly order below.</Callout>

Assemble the parts in this order, from the CO₂ supply down to the tank:

<Steps>
  <Step title="CO₂ connection">
    Ideally an industrial push-to-connect fitting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Isolation valve">
    Install so that the handle covers the CO₂ connection when the valve is in the **On** position.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check valve">
    Mounted vertically, directly below the isolation valve.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Elbow">
    Turns the line toward the tank port.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Carbonation stone">
    Connected through the elbow to the tank.
  </Step>
</Steps>

To check the isolation valve and stone once installed, see [Check 7: Stone/Isolation Valve](/health-check/check-7-stone-isolation-valve).

## Replacing the duck bill valve

<Steps>
  <Step title="Remove the inner plug">
    Hold the stainless steel outer body and unscrew the inner plug with a wrench.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove the old duck bill valve">
    Take out the worn or damaged duck bill valve.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Clean the parts">
    Wash the inner plug and outer body with detergent, then rinse and dry them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fit the new duck bill valve">
    Seat the new duck bill valve in place.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reassemble">
    Apply thread lock to the inner plug threads and screw it back into the outer body. Tighten by hand, then let the thread lock set. There is no need to clamp the body in a vice.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Chemical compatibility

The ratings below are for the Delrin (acetal) inner plug, based on 48-hour exposure tests. Delrin is widely used in food and beverage equipment and has excellent resistance to most organic compounds.

### Caustic cleaner test

The inner plug was exposed for 48 hours to a common caustic cleaner at 60 to 70 °C, mixed at 30 g per 1 L of water (twice the manufacturer's recommended dose). It showed **no damage or discolouration**.

The cleaner contained:

- Sodium metasilicate
- Sodium carbonate peroxide
- Sodium carbonate
- Tetrasodium ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid tetrahydrate
- Pentasodium triphosphate
- Isopropanol

### Other ratings

| Substance | Rating |
| --- | --- |
| Beer, cider, wine, coffee, rum and spirits | Excellent |
| Pure neat juices and syrups | Good to excellent |
| Acid cleaner | Use at a mixing ratio of 5% or lower, and rinse after exposure |

<Callout kind="danger">Do not use alcohol-based cleaners. Some alcohol products can cause the Delrin inner plug to crack.</Callout>
