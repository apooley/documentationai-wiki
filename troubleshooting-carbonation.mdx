## Troubleshooting carbonation and brite pressure problems

Expand the section that matches your symptom, then work through the steps in order.

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="Carbonation is well below target" default-open="false">
    <Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
      Example: you set 2.7 volumes and achieve 2.1.
    </Callout>

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Check brite starting pressure" title-type="p">
        The brite tank should start close to zero psi. If the tank is already pressurized, the differential pressure driving CO₂ into solution is reduced, and carbonation will be lower than expected.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Check CO₂ flow rates" title-type="p">
        Use the BrewWizz app flow rate calculator to verify that flow rates match expectations for your tank volume and target volumes.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Suspect a hydrophobic carbonation stone" title-type="p">
        A dirty stone repels water, reducing diffusion efficiency. Clean the stone using a torpedo or another appropriate cleaning method. Blocked or dirty stones are a leading cause of low carbonation.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Check the temperature sensor" title-type="p">
        Carbonation is temperature-dependent. Run the [Slushy WizzTemp Test](/testing/temperature-probe) to confirm the probe is accurate, and check Ident C on the [Results Form](/health-check/results-form).
      </Step>

      <Step title="Brite pressure building very quickly" title-type="p">
        Check for these two causes:

        - A leaking Head\_Transfer solenoid. Confirm this with [Check 6 — Leak Testing](/health-check/check-6-leak-testing) and review Ident U.
        - A broken or damaged carbonation stone. Gas can bypass the diffusion surface entirely.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Brite tank not building up pressure" default-open="false">
    This is almost always a leak. Work through these steps in order:

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Run Checks 6 and 7" title-type="p">
        Start with [Check 6 — Leak Testing](/health-check/check-6-leak-testing) and [Check 7 — Stone/Isolation Valve](/health-check/check-7-stone-isolation-valve) to isolate the circuit that is leaking.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Check the stone hose for leaks" title-type="p">
        Inspect the stone hose closely, especially at the push-to-connect fittings. These fittings are a common source of small leaks.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Check the stone modification kit" title-type="p">
        Inspect the modification kit connections for any signs of gas escaping.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Check the arm hose and separator" title-type="p">
        Make sure the swiffer valve on the separator is fully closed and not leaking. If the arm circuit will not hold pressure, check the swiffer valve first.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Stone maintenance" default-open="false">
    <Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
      Carbonation stones cause a disproportionate share of carbonation complaints. A regular cleaning schedule prevents hydrophobic buildup and most stone-related issues. Clean the stone with a torpedo or another appropriate cleaning method as part of routine maintenance.
    </Callout>
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>
