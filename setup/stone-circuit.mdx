## Stone circuit

The stone circuit is where CO₂ enters the beer. Gas leaves the FizzWizz through the stone hose, passes a series of one-way valves, and diffuses into the tank through the carbonation stone.

This is the circuit most exposed to product, so the valves that protect it — and the fittings that seal it — matter as much as the stone itself.

## The circuit path

| Order | Component | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **CO₂-in solenoid** (inside the unit) | Opens gas flow to the stone |
| 2 | **Flow controller** (inside the unit) | Meters the flow rate. The ball indicates rate |
| 3 | **Stone hose** | 5/16 inch ID NSF tubing from the unit to the stone assembly |
| 4 | **Inline check valve** | One-way valve in the hose — blocks liquid travelling back toward the unit |
| 5 | **Stone Isolation Valve** | Manual valve that isolates the stone. Used in Check 7 |
| 6 | **Sanitary Check Valve** | Second one-way valve, immediately before the stone on the wetted side |
| 7 | **Carbonation stone** | Porous ceramic diffusion element inside the tank |

See the [Pneumatics Schematic](/get-started/pneumatics-schematic) for the diagram and the full component glossary.

## Connecting the stone hose

<Callout kind="danger">

Power the FizzWizz **OFF** before connecting or disconnecting any hoses.

</Callout>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Power off">

  Switch the FizzWizz OFF.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect the hose to the unit">

  Fit the stone hose to the STONE connection on the FizzWizz.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Fit the inline check valve">

  Install the inline check valve in the stone hose, oriented to block flow back toward the unit. Check the flow direction arrow if one is marked.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Route the hose to the stone assembly">

  Route the hose to the tank, avoiding tight bends and anywhere it can be stood on or crushed.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Assemble the stone end">

  Fit the isolation valve, check valve, elbow, and carbonation stone in the correct order. The [Check Valve](/setup/check-valve#installation) page has the assembly order, including the vertical mounting requirement.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Fit the stone into the tank">

  Install the carbonation stone in the tank port. See [WizzStone-Combo](/setup/wizzstone-combo) for assembly of the carrier and stone.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Push-to-connect fittings

The stone circuit uses push-to-connect fittings, including a ¼ in NPT pneumatic push fitting where the hose joins the stone assembly.

<Callout kind="alert">

**Push-to-connect fittings are a common source of small leaks.** Check these first when the flow meter ball behaves oddly, when the stone circuit will not hold pressure, or when a tank will not build pressure. A small leak here is easy to miss and produces symptoms that look like a component failure.

</Callout>

To test a suspect fitting, spray it with soapy water and look for bubbles. This is the first step for Health Check **Ident X** and part of the diagnosis for **Ident V**.

## The isolation valve

The stone isolation valve is a manual valve that isolates the carbonation stone from the rest of the circuit. It is used to prove the stone circuit holds pressure in [Check 7](/troubleshooting/health-check/check-7-stone-isolation-valve).

| Valve position | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Open** | Normal operation — gas flows to the stone |
| **Closed** | Isolates the stone. Used to test whether the circuit holds |

During Check 7, the flow meter ball should be adjustable with the valve open, and should drop when the valve is closed. If it does not drop, there is a leak between the FizzWizz and the isolation valve — check the hose and push fittings.

## Carbonation stones

The stone is a porous ceramic element. The WizzStone-Combo uses a 16 in stone with 0.5 micron pores, producing very fine bubbles and high CO₂ transfer.

Because the pores are small, the stone clogs easily and **must be cleaned after every use**. A dirty stone becomes hydrophobic — it repels water rather than diffusing gas — and is a leading cause of low carbonation.

See [WizzStone-Combo cleaning](/setup/wizzstone-combo#cleaning) for the torpedo and ultrasonic methods.

## Setting the flow rate

The flow controller on the side of the unit sets how fast CO₂ enters the stone. The correct rate depends on stone length, porosity, product height over the stone, and finish gravity.

Use the BrewWizz app's CO₂ Stone flow rate calculator — see [Setting the CO₂ flow rate](/setup/wizzstone-combo#setting-the-co-flow-rate). The WizzStone-Combo supports up to 16 CFH.

## Checking the circuit

After connecting, run [Check 7 — Stone/Isolation Valve](/troubleshooting/health-check/check-7-stone-isolation-valve) to confirm the circuit holds. If carbonation is low and the Health Check passes, see [Carbonation & Brite Pressure](/troubleshooting/carbonation-and-brite-pressure).
