## CO₂ supply connection

The FizzWizz takes CO₂ from your regulator into the `CO₂_In` port. This is the first pneumatic connection to make, and the pressure you set here is the reference for everything downstream.

## Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Regulator setting | **30–40 psi** |
| Connection point | The `CO₂_In` port on the unit |
| Supply | Sized for your batch volume and target carbonation — see the [flow rate calculator](/setup/wizzstone-combo#setting-the-co-flow-rate) |

## Connecting the supply

<Callout kind="danger">

Power the FizzWizz **OFF** before connecting or disconnecting any hoses.

</Callout>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Power off">

  Switch the FizzWizz OFF.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect CO₂ to CO₂_In">

  Connect the CO₂ supply from your regulator to the `CO₂_In` port.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the regulator">

  Set the CO₂ regulator to approximately **30–40 psi**.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Check for leaks">

  With the supply open, check the connection for leaks. A soapy water solution on the fitting will show bubbles at a leak.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## The CO₂ inlet circuit

From the `CO₂_In` port, gas passes the **CO₂ check valve** and enters the internal manifold, which distributes it to the three solenoids.

The CO₂ check valve is a one-way valve that prevents reverse flow back toward the regulator if the supply runs out. It matters more than it looks: if it fails or is missing, and the CO₂ supply runs out, product can flow back from the tank toward your regulator.

See the [Pneumatics Schematic](/get-started/pneumatics-schematic) for the full path and component glossary.

## Reading the pressure

Once the unit is powered, **CO₂ reg psi** on the display shows the pressure at this inlet. In [Test Mode](/troubleshooting/health-check/test-mode) it updates live.

A `CO₂ reg psi` reading that does not match your regulator setting is Health Check **Ident A** (or **Ident R** on the ARM circuit) — see [By Symptom](/troubleshooting/by-symptom).

## Common problems

| Symptom | Likely cause | Where to go |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display reading differs from the regulator | Faulty CO₂_In pressure transducer, or a regulator problem | Ident A / R — [By Symptom](/troubleshooting/by-symptom) |
| Pressure drops during a run | CO₂ supply running low | [Responding to Alerts](/troubleshooting/responding-to-alerts) |
| Gas escaping at the fitting | Loose connection, or a failed push-to-connect fitting | [Leak testing](/troubleshooting/health-check/check-6-leak-testing) |
| No flow into the stone circuit despite pressure at the inlet | CO₂-in solenoid stuck closed, or the flow controller is closed | Ident I — [Check 3](/troubleshooting/health-check/check-3-solenoid-co2-in) |

<Callout kind="tip">

If flow appears to be blocked, **check the flow controller first**. A flow meter turned all the way off looks exactly like a stuck solenoid. Rotate the knob counterclockwise and retest before suspecting a component — this is the first action for Ident I and Ident P.

</Callout>

## During the Health Check

The Health Check moves the CO₂ supply between ports to isolate faults:

- Checks 2 and 3 use CO₂ connected to `CO₂_In`
- Check 4 moves CO₂ to the **ARM** port and repeats the solenoid tests — the expected results swap, which is the point of the test
- Checks 6 and 7 move CO₂ back to `CO₂_In`

A push-to-connect jumper hose makes these moves quick. See the [Health Check Overview](/troubleshooting/health-check/overview) for the prerequisites.
