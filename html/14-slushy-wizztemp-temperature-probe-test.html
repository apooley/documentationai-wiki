The Health Check only confirms the temperature reading is plausible. This test confirms it is accurate, by measuring against a known physical reference.

An ice-and-water slushy sits at 0 °C (32 °F) at the interface between the ice and the water, for as long as both are present. That makes it a reliable calibration point requiring no special equipment.

The FizzWizz must be in Test Mode.

Step 1: Make a slushy

  1. Fill the whole container with crushed ice — as finely crushed as possible.
  2. Pour in approximately 20% additional tap water.
  3. Don't worry if water overflows, but keep the ice.

The aim is to fill every gap between the ice with water. The interface between ice and water is 0 °C / 32 °F.

Step 2: Agitate the sensor continuously

Continually and vigorously move the sensor in the slushy, like mixing a cup of tea. After 2 to 3 minutes check the reading — but keep moving the sensor. Never stop during the test.

Agitation is not optional. A stationary probe forms a thin layer of slightly warmer meltwater around itself and will read high, which is the most common reason this test appears to fail on a healthy probe.

Expected results

ReadingInterpretation
0.25 °C / 32.45 °F or lower Pass. The exact value depends on impurities in the water used.
Above 0.25 °C / 32.45 °F Fail — reading high. First confirm you agitated continuously and the slushy still contained plenty of ice. If so, suspect the probe.
Below −0.16 °C / 31.71 °F Fail — reading impossibly low. It should not be possible to go below this value in an ice-water slushy. A lower reading indicates a faulty probe or loom.

For more accurate temperature testing, use deionized water — dissolved minerals in tap water depress the freezing point slightly.

If the test fails

  1. Repeat the test properly first. Fresh slushy, plenty of ice, continuous vigorous agitation. Most failures are method problems.
  2. Inspect the temperature sensor internal loom — a marginal connection produces drifting or offset readings.
  3. Change the WizzTemp sensor.

See Ident C in Troubleshooting by Symptom.

Why this matters for carbonation

Carbonation volumes are calculated from temperature. A probe reading warm will cause the FizzWizz to systematically under-carbonate, which often shows up as the complaint covered in Troubleshooting Low Carbonation. If carbonation is off target and the stone is clean, verify the probe here before replacing anything else.
