The Health Check is a structured test sequence that confirms every element of the FizzWizz™ is operating correctly and identifies any maintenance requirements before they affect a brew.

It walks through seven checks covering the power supply, the display readings, all three internal solenoids, the arm circuit and the stone circuit. Each individual test result is given a letter ID from A to X.

When to run a Health Check

  • When commissioning a new or relocated FizzWizz
  • As part of scheduled preventative maintenance
  • Before returning a unit to service after a repair
  • When carbonation results are off target — for example, set to 2.7 volumes but achieving 2.1
  • Whenever you suspect a leak, a stuck solenoid or a faulty sensor

The A–X ID system

The letter IDs are the single most useful thing to understand about this documentation set. The same letter means the same test in all three documents:

  • This procedure tells you how to perform test C and what a good result looks like.
  • The results form gives you a row to record the outcome of test C.
  • The troubleshooting guide tells you what to do when test C fails.

So if a unit fails at C, you go straight to Ident C in the troubleshooting guide. No hunting.

The seven checks

CheckCoversIDs
1Setup and wall-wart power supply—
2CO2_In display valuesA–C
3Solenoid function, CO2_InD–J
4Solenoid function, ARMK–Q
5ARM display valuesR–T
6Gloved finger and separator integrityU–V
7Stone hose connections and isolation valveW–X

Work through them in order. Checks 4, 6 and 7 depend on the CO2 connection being moved, so skipping around will give you misleading results.

What you need before you start

  • A CO2 supply with a regulator that can be set to 30–40 psi
  • The FizzWizz wall-wart power supply
  • The stone hose and a carbonation stone (needed for Check 7)
  • The arm hose with separator fitted (needed for Check 6)
  • A push-to-connect jumper hose for moving CO2 between ports
  • A disposable glove (needed for Check 6)
  • A printed Health Test Results Form

Before you begin: the most common root cause

Faults affecting pressure transducers and solenoids are typically caused by fluid getting into the FizzWizz. This happens when the arm separator is not used or is overwhelmed, or when check valves are stuck open or were never installed.

If a unit fails several unrelated tests at once, suspect fluid ingress before you start replacing individual components.

Record your results

Fill in the Health Test Results Form as you go rather than from memory. Record the actual measured values, not just pass or fail — a reading that passes today but has drifted since the last check is useful early warning, and it is the first thing support will ask for.
