These are performance problems rather than component failures. A unit can pass every check in the Health Check and still under-carbonate, so this article covers the causes that a pass/fail test will not catch.

Carbonation is well below target

For example: set to 2.7 volumes, achieving only 2.1.

1. Check the brite starting pressure

The brite starting pressure should be close to zero psi. Starting from a pressurised tank changes the whole carbonation calculation and is the first thing to rule out.

2. Check the CO2 flow rates

Use the flow rate calculator in the BrewWizz app to confirm the actual flow matches expectations. A flow rate well below what the setting calls for points to a restricted stone.

3. Suspect a hydrophobic carbonation stone

A dirty carbonation stone becomes hydrophobic — it repels water rather than diffusing gas into it, so CO2 passes through without dissolving properly. This is one of the most common causes of persistent under-carbonation.

Fix: use a torpedo to clean the carbonation stone.

4. Check the temperature sensor

Carbonation is temperature-dependent, so a probe reading a few degrees warm will systematically under-carbonate. Run the Slushy WizzTemp Test and check C in the Health Check.

5. Brite pressure building very quickly

If brite pressure rises faster than expected, gas is reaching the tank head without going through the stone. Two causes:

  • A leaking head transfer solenoid — confirm with check U in Check 6.
  • A broken or damaged carbonation stone — gas is bypassing the diffusion surface entirely.

Brite tank not building up pressure

This is the opposite symptom and is almost always a leak. Work through these:

  1. Run Checks 6 and 7 from the Health Check — see Check 6 and Check 7. These are designed to find exactly this.
  2. Check for a leak on the stone hose, especially at the pneumatic push fittings.
  3. Check for a leak on the stone modification kit.
  4. Check for a leak on the arm hose and separator, including the swiffer valve.

A note on stone maintenance

Carbonation stones account for a disproportionate share of performance complaints, in both directions — a blocked or hydrophobic stone under-carbonates, and a cracked stone lets gas bypass the water entirely. Cleaning stones on a regular schedule prevents most of these problems before they start.
