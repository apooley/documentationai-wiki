This is the reference diagram for everything downstream of your CO2 regulator. Most of the Health Check and troubleshooting articles refer back to the component names used here.

The three circuits

CircuitPathWhat it does
CO2 inlet Regulator → CO2 check valve → manifold Supplies gas to everything else. Tested in Check 2.
Stone (carbonation) CO2-in solenoid → flow controller → stone hose → inline check valve → isolation valve → sanitary check valve → carbonation stone Delivers metered CO2 into the tank through the stone. Tested in Check 7.
Arm (head transfer) Head_Transfer solenoid → arm hose → separator → tank head Applies and senses head pressure. Tested in Check 6.

Component glossary

Inside the enclosure

ComponentDescription
Flow ControllerThe vertical flow meter with the adjustment knob. The ball indicates CO2 flow rate into the stone circuit. Rotate the knob counterclockwise to open.
Head_Transfer SolenoidControls gas to the ARM connection. Operated by the Up button in test mode.
CO2-in SolenoidControls gas to the STONE connection. Operated by the Down button in test mode.
Vent SolenoidControls gas to the vent valve. Operated by the Enter button in test mode.
Internal FilterProtects the solenoids and transducers from particulate carried in the gas stream.
Vent ValveThe manual valve on the manifold that releases pressure to atmosphere. Must be open for the vent test in Check 4.
CO2 check valveOne-way valve on the CO2 inlet. Prevents reverse flow back toward the regulator if the supply runs out.

Stone circuit

ComponentDescription
Stone hose5/16 inch ID NSF tubing running from the FizzWizz to the stone assembly.
Inline check valveOne-way valve in the stone hose. Blocks liquid travelling back toward the unit.
1/4 inch NPT pneumatic push fittingThreaded fitting joining the hose to the stone assembly.
Push-to-connect fittingQuick-release connector. A common source of small leaks — check these first when the flow meter ball behaves oddly.
Stone Isolation ValveManual valve that isolates the carbonation stone. Used to prove the stone circuit holds in Check 7.
Sanitary Check ValveSecond one-way valve immediately before the stone, on the wetted side.
ElbowRight-angle fitting connecting the check valve assembly to the stone.
Carbonation stoneThe diffusion stone itself. Can be left in the tank. Blocked or dirty stones are a leading cause of low carbonation.

Arm circuit

ComponentDescription
Arm HoseConnects the ARM port to the tank head.
SeparatorCatches liquid and foam before it can reach the FizzWizz. The single most important component for protecting the unit.
Swiffer valveDrain valve on the separator. Must be closed during Check 6.2, and is the first thing to check if the arm circuit will not hold pressure.

Why the check valves and separator matter

Pressure transducer and solenoid failures are usually not component defects — they are the downstream consequence of fluid reaching parts that were never meant to see it. The separator, the inline check valve and the sanitary check valve exist solely to prevent this.

If you find fluid inside the unit, replacing the failed transducer or solenoid without also establishing how the fluid got in will simply produce the same failure again.
