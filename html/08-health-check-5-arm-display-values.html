Check 5 re-reads the same three display values as Check 2, but with CO2 supplied to the ARM port.

This matters because in Check 2 the arm circuit was open to atmosphere, so Brite psi read zero. A transducer that is stuck at zero would have passed. Here the arm circuit is pressurised, so Brite psi should now read a real pressure — which is what proves the transducer is actually responding.

Preconditions

  • CO2 still connected to ARM from Check 4
  • Regulator at 30–40 psi
  • FizzWizz in Test Mode

The checks

IDDisplay valueCriterionTolerance
R CO2 reg psi Equals the supply regulator dial, and falls between 30 and 40 psi ±3 psi
S Brite psi Equals the CO2 reg psi reading, and falls between 30 and 40 psi ±3 psi
T Temperature A representative value, similar to the reading recorded at check C Judgement

Reading S: the key measurement

With gas fed into the ARM port, the arm circuit is pressurised to supply pressure. Brite psi and CO2 reg psi should therefore agree within 3 psi.

If Brite psi still reads near zero, the Brite transducer is not responding — change it and inspect for fluid in the pipe leading to the transducers.

Reading T: compare against C

Temperature should be close to whatever you recorded at check C. A significant change between the two readings, taken minutes apart in the same location, points to an intermittent sensor or a loom fault.
