Check 7 tests the stone circuit — the path that actually delivers CO2 into your tank. It confirms the flow controller adjusts across its range and that the circuit seals when the isolation valve is closed.

This check runs in Carbonation mode, not Test Mode. It is the only part of the Health Check that does.

Step 7: Setup

  1. Connect a carbonation stone to the stone hose. The stone can be in the tank.
  2. Put the FizzWizz into Carbonation mode.

Check 7.1: Isolation valve OPEN (W)

With the isolation valve open, rotate the knob on the flow controller and observe the flow indicator ball.

IDCriterionPass
WBall is fully adjustable up and down across the range of the knobFull adjustment

If the ball stays down

  1. Confirm the flow meter is not turned fully off — rotate the knob counterclockwise and retest.
  2. Suspect a blocked carbonation stone.
  3. Check the sanitary and inline check valves.

If the ball stays up

Check the stone hose for leaks, especially around the pneumatic push fittings.

Check 7.2: Isolation valve CLOSED (X)

Close the carbonation stone isolation valve and observe the flow indicator ball.

IDCriterionPass
XBall drops fully to the bottom of the meter, indicating no flowBall at zero

Documentation discrepancy — pending confirmation. The Health Test Procedure states the ball should be fully at the bottom after approximately 15 seconds. The Results Form states approximately 10 seconds. Allow the longer period of 15 seconds before recording a failure until this is resolved.

With the stone isolated, no gas can escape, so flow must fall to zero. A ball that keeps floating means gas is still going somewhere it should not — a leak downstream of the flow meter but upstream of the isolation valve.

If the ball does not drop to zero

Check the stone hose and all push fittings between the FizzWizz and the isolation valve for leaks.

Health Check complete

That is the last check. Complete and file the Health Test Results Form.

If any check failed, work through Troubleshooting by Symptom using the ID letters you recorded. If all checks passed but carbonation performance is still poor, see Troubleshooting Low Carbonation and Brite Pressure Problems.
