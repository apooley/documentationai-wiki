Check 6 is a pair of leak tests on the arm circuit. They are the most sensitive tests in the Health Check, and the ones most likely to reveal a slow fault that has not yet caused an obvious symptom.

Step 6: Reconnect CO2 to CO2_In

  1. Disconnect the push-to-connect from the ARM connection and re-attach it to CO2_In.
  2. Allow the values to settle for 2 minutes.

Do not rush the settling time. Starting either test before readings are stable is the most common cause of a false failure here.

Check 6.1: Gloved finger test (U)

Using a gloved hand, cover and plug the ARM connection. Observe the Brite pressure for 30 seconds.

IDCriterionPass
U Brite pressure should not increase by more than 0.10 psi over 30 seconds Increase < 0.10 psi

What this test is looking for: CO2 is on the CO2_In port and the Head_Transfer solenoid should be closed, so the sealed arm circuit should hold steady. A rising Brite pressure means gas is seeping past the Head_Transfer solenoid.

Documentation discrepancy — pending confirmation. The Health Test Procedure specifies a pressure increase of no more than 0.10 psi, and the troubleshooting guide agrees (“pressure increased on finger test” indicates a leaking Head_Transfer solenoid). However, the Results Form records this row as Start − Finish, which computes a pressure drop. Record Finish − Start for this row until the form is corrected.

If U fails

Replace the Head_Transfer solenoid — it has a small leak.

Check 6.2: Arm hose and separator test (V)

  1. Connect the arm hose with separator to the ARM connection.
  2. Ensure the swiffer valve is closed.
  3. Press the Up button for one pulse. This squirts CO2 into the arm and pressurises the circuit.
  4. Observe the Brite pressure for 30 seconds.
IDCriterionPass
V Brite pressure should not drop by more than 1.5 psi over 30 seconds Drop ≤ 1.5 psi

Record the start and finish pressures, and the difference. In the example readings above, one pulse took the circuit from 0.001 to 13.277 psi — the absolute value is not important, the rate of decay is.

If V fails

Work through these in order — the first two cost nothing to check:

  1. Check the swiffer valve. An unclosed or worn swiffer valve is the most common cause.
  2. Remove the separator and recheck. If the leak disappears, the separator is at fault.
  3. Suspect a leak in the arm hose or separator itself — check fittings and connections along the whole run.
  4. Check the vent solenoid. A vent solenoid stuck open will bleed the circuit; see Ident M in the troubleshooting guide.
