## Check 6 — Leak testing

Verify the arm circuit holds pressure with two leak tests — a sealed-port pressure hold and a separator-equipped decay test. These are the most sensitive tests in the Health Check, and the ones most likely to reveal a slow fault that has not yet caused an obvious symptom.

## Preconditions

<Callout kind="info">

- [Check 5](/health-check/check-5-arm-display-values) passed
- FizzWizz in [Test Mode](/health-check/test-mode)

</Callout>

## Step 6: Reconnect CO₂ to CO₂_In

<Steps>
  <Step title="Disconnect from ARM">

  Disconnect the push-to-connect from the ARM connection.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Reconnect to CO₂_In">

  Re-attach it to the CO₂_In port.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Allow to settle">

  Wait 2 minutes for pressure readings to stabilize before starting either test.

  </Step>
</Steps>

Do not rush the settling time. Starting either test before readings are stable is the most common cause of a false failure here.

## Check 6.1 — Gloved finger test (Ident U)

Using a gloved hand, cover and plug the ARM connection to seal the arm circuit. Observe the Brite pressure reading for 30 seconds.

![Gloved finger test](/images/gloved-finger-test.png)

*Plug the ARM connection with a gloved finger and watch the Brite pressure.*

| Ident | Criterion | Pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **U** | Brite pressure should not increase by more than 0.10 psi over 30 seconds | Increase < 0.10 psi |

## What the gloved finger test looks for

With CO₂ on the CO₂_In port, the Head_Transfer solenoid should be closed and the sealed arm circuit should hold steady. A rising Brite pressure means gas is seeping past the Head_Transfer solenoid.

## Documentation discrepancy

<Callout kind="alert">

The Health Test Procedure specifies a pressure **increase** of no more than 0.10 psi, and the troubleshooting guide agrees. "Pressure increased on finger test" indicates a leaking Head_Transfer solenoid. However, the Results Form records this row as Start − Finish, which computes a pressure **drop**. Record **Finish − Start** for this row until the form is corrected, so a positive value indicates the pressure rise the test is looking for.

</Callout>

## If U fails

Replace the Head_Transfer solenoid — it has a small leak past the seat.

## Check 6.2 — Arm hose and separator test (Ident V)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect arm hose with separator">

  Attach the arm hose with the separator fitted to the ARM connection.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Close swiffer valve">

  Ensure the swiffer drain valve on the separator is closed.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Press Up for one pulse">

  Briefly press the Up button to pressurize the arm circuit with one pulse of CO₂.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Observe for 30 seconds">

  Watch the Brite pressure for 30 seconds. It should not drop by more than 1.5 psi.

  </Step>
</Steps>

![Arm circuit separator](/images/arm-circuit-separator.png)

*The arm circuit under test, with typical before and after pulse readings.*

| Ident | Criterion | Pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **V** | Brite pressure should not drop by more than 1.5 psi over 30 seconds | Drop ≤ 1.5 psi |

## Recording V results

Record the start pressure, finish pressure, and the difference, Start − Finish, on the [Results Form](/health-check/results-form). In the example readings, one pulse took the circuit from `0.001` to `13.277` psi — the absolute value is not important, the rate of decay is.

## If V fails

1. **Check the swiffer valve.** An unclosed or worn swiffer valve is the most common cause.
2. **Remove the separator and recheck.** If the leak disappears, the separator is at fault.
3. **Suspect a leak in the arm hose or separator** itself — check fittings and connections along the whole run.
4. **Check the vent solenoid.** A vent solenoid stuck open will bleed the circuit — see Ident M in [Troubleshooting by Symptom](/troubleshooting-guide).
