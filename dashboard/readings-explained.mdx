## Understanding your readings

The dashboard and the FizzWizz display show three core values. Understanding what each one represents makes it much easier to tell a normal run from a problem.

## The three core readings

| Reading | What it measures | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CO₂ reg psi** | Pressure at the CO₂ inlet port | CO₂_In pressure transducer |
| **Brite psi** | Pressure in the arm/head transfer circuit — the tank head space pressure | Brite pressure transducer |
| **Temperature** | Product temperature | WizzTemp sensor |

All three are visible on the FizzWizz display, in [Test Mode](/troubleshooting/health-check/test-mode), and on the dashboard.

## CO₂ reg psi

**What it should be:** 30–40 psi, matching your regulator setting, and **steady**.

This is your supply pressure. It tells you how much CO₂ is available to push into the tank.

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Steady 30–40 psi | Normal |
| Slowly declining | CO₂ supply running low — plan a cylinder change |
| Well below the regulator setting | Regulator problem, or a failed CO₂_In transducer |
| Not matching the regulator at all | Ident A — [By Symptom](/troubleshooting/by-symptom) |

<Callout kind="tip">

A declining CO₂ reg psi is one of the alerts the FizzWizz raises. Running out mid-batch stalls carbonation, and the batch then has to be rested so CO₂ can re-dissolve at the right equilibrium pressure.

</Callout>

## Brite psi

**What it should be:** climbing steadily toward the finish pressure during carbonation, then holding.

This is the pressure in the tank head space — the pressure actually driving CO₂ into solution.

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Not near zero at rest | Brite transducer issue, or fluid inside the unit — Ident B |
| Climbing steadily toward target | Normal carbonation in progress |
| Flat, never climbing | A leak — see [Carbonation & Brite Pressure](/troubleshooting/carbonation-and-brite-pressure) |
| Climbing unusually fast | Leaking Head_Transfer solenoid, or a broken stone — Ident U |
| Not matching CO₂ reg psi when expected to | Brite transducer, or fluid inside the unit — Ident S |

### Why Brite psi alone is not the whole story

Carbonation depends on **both** pressure and temperature. The FizzWizz calculates the finish pressure required for your target at the current temperature — so the same Brite psi means different carbonation at different temperatures.

This is why the unit recalculates continuously, and why a temperature error causes systematic carbonation error even when pressure looks perfect.

## Temperature

**What it should be:** stable, and **accurate**.

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Stable at the product setpoint | Normal |
| Drifting or stepping | Probe, loom, or glycol system — Ident C / T |
| Implausible (impossibly low or high) | Faulty probe or internal loom |
| Plausible but inaccurate | Causes systematic under- or over-carbonation — run the [Slushy WizzTemp Test](/troubleshooting/temperature-probe) |

<Callout kind="alert">

Carbonation volumes are calculated from temperature. A probe that reads **warm** makes the FizzWizz calculate *less* CO₂ than needed, and the beer is systematically under-carbonated — while every pressure reading looks correct. This is one of the hardest faults to spot from the dashboard alone, and one of the most common causes of persistent under-carbonation.

</Callout>

## During packaging

During kegging, canning, or bottling, the important reading is the pressure the **packaging line** sees. If you have a [Foot Pressure Sensor](/setup/foot-pressure-sensor) fitted at the tank foot, that is the value to watch.

The foot pressure must stay at or above the finish pressure, or CO₂ starts coming out of solution. Once breakout starts it tends to run away and is difficult to stop.

**Minimum foot pressure = (product depth in ft × 0.43 psi) + finish pressure**

See [Choosing a foot pressure](/setup/foot-pressure-sensor#choosing-a-foot-pressure) for worked examples.
